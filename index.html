    Section 01

    Exam Dates & Key Facts

    ⚠ Critical Timeline Note
    TIFR GS results are declared in December so the TIFR written exam is in Month 8 of your cycle (typically December) — not the same window as JAM and JEST. JAM falls in Month 9 and JEST in Month 10. These are separate exam windows and the plan respects them. See Phase 4 buffer for TIFR exam week.
    Exam Typical Window Format Duration Key Weights

    TIFR GS (I-PhD) Typically Month 8 · Early Dec Section A (MCQ) + Section B (Short) + Section C (Essay) 3 hrs QM 30%, Stat Mech 20%, EM 20%, CM 15%, Math 15%. Conceptual depth prioritised

    Theory Phase Rule
    After completing every subtopic listed below, immediately solve 1–2 PYQ questions on that subtopic before moving forward. This is the most important habit of the theory phase — it anchors concepts and identifies gaps while they're fresh. Keep a running formula sheet per topic.
    Note

    Thermodynamics & Stat Mech — Handled in the Problem Phase

    Thermodynamics and Statistical Mechanics is deliberately placed in the Problem Phase (Months 5–6) rather than the Theory Phase. Here's why:

    • The Theory Phase is already at capacity with Math, CM, EM, and QM. Adding Stat Mech would require compressing a priority topic.
    • Stat Mech heavily borrows from QM (quantum statistics, partition functions) — learning it after QM is pedagogically better.
    • The exam weights: Stat Mech is 18% of JEST and 22% of TIFR — important but not at the same level as QM.
    • In the Problem Phase (Month 5), the plan allocates the first 3 weeks to Stat Mech theory + problems simultaneously.

    Key Strategy Tips

    JEST/TIFR vs JAM Mindset
    JAM tests breadth — cover all topics including Solid State and Electronics. JEST/TIFR test depth — they probe conceptual understanding rigorously, especially in QM and Stat Mech. Your plan covers both: theory phases build depth, JAM-specific phases ensure breadth.
    • After every theory subtopic, solve 1–2 PYQ questions immediately — this is non-negotiable in the theory phase.
    • Build a formula sheet for each major topic as you go. By Phase 5, you should have ~8–10 condensed pages total.
    • Use spaced repetition for formulas — review yesterday's topic for 15 min every morning before starting new material.
    • TIFR Section B/C questions require deeper physical reasoning — practice writing short conceptual explanations, not just calculations.
    • In Phase 5, maintain an error log: classify every wrong answer as (a) conceptual gap, (b) formula error, or (c) careless mistake.
    • For JEST: Quantum Mechanics alone is worth ~25–30% — allocate maximum energy there. Never sacrifice QM depth for other topics.
    • Sunday is your mandatory rest + light review day every week throughout the 10 months. Do not skip rest.
    • In the last 2 weeks before each exam, do not attempt new topics or unfamiliar problems — only revise what you know.
